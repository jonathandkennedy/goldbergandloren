Hurt in a Grapevine Car Wreck? Do These 5 Things

What you do in the first days protects your claim. If you can't do all of this, call us and we'll handle it.

1

Get medical care now

Go to the ER or urgent care, for example Baylor Scott & White Medical Center – Grapevine, even if you feel "okay." Insurers use gaps in treatment to cut offers.

2

Get the crash report

Grapevine Police (or Texas DPS on SH-114/SH-121) files a Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report (CR-3). Copies are also sold through TxDOT's online system.1 We request it for you.

3

Photograph everything

Vehicles, plates, the intersection, signals and your injuries. Ask nearby businesses about camera footage, because it is often erased within days.

4

Don't give a recorded statement

The other driver's adjuster works for their insurer. Talk to a lawyer before you give a statement or sign anything.

5

Don't accept the first offer

First offers are usually low. Once you sign a release, the claim is over, even if your injuries get worse.

We front every cost

Crash reports, records, experts, filing fees. No recovery, no attorney fee, and you're never out of pocket while your case is open.

Grapevine Car Accident FAQs

How long do I have to file a car accident claim in Texas?

In most Texas injury cases, the deadline to file a lawsuit is two years from the date of the crash.7 Claims involving a city, county or other government vehicle can require written notice much sooner. Evidence such as camera footage disappears far sooner, so it pays to start early. Legal review

What if I was partly at fault?

Texas uses proportionate responsibility: you can recover as long as you are not more than 50% responsible, and your recovery is reduced by your share of fault.8 Don't accept the insurer's version of fault. Get a free review first. Legal review

Where would my Grapevine case be filed?

Most claims settle without a lawsuit. If one is needed, Texas venue rules generally allow filing in the county where the crash happened or where the defendant lives.9 For a crash in Grapevine, that is usually the Tom Vandergriff Civil Courts Building (Tarrant County civil district courts), 100 N. Calhoun St., Fort Worth (Dallas or Denton County for the small parts of town in those counties).2 Legal review

I was in town for a flight or a convention and got hurt. Can I still bring a Grapevine case?

Yes. A Texas claim follows where the crash happened, not where you live. We can handle most of the case by phone and video after you fly home, and we can coordinate with your doctors where you live.

The insurance company already offered me money. Should I take it?

Not before a free case review. First offers are usually low, and signing a release normally ends your claim, even if your injuries get worse.

What if the driver who hit me had no insurance?

Your own uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) or personal injury protection (PIP) coverage may apply. We review your policy for free.
